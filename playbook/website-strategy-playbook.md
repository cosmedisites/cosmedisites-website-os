# CosmediSites Website Strategy Playbook v1.0

## Purpose

This playbook explains **how CosmediSites thinks about clinic website redesigns**.

It is not the day-to-day project checklist. The operational sequence lives in the Website Redesign SOP. The templates and AI workflow pack are the execution layer.

The objective of a CosmediSites redesign is to create a website that is:

**Commercially useful → strategically positioned → visually coherent → conversion-led → scalable → buildable**

We are not selling "a nicer website".

We are creating a website system that improves how the clinic is:

**understood → trusted → explored → chosen → contacted**

The final client presentation should make the redesign feel like the inevitable result of the strategy, not one designer's subjective taste.

---

# 1. The CosmediSites Redesign Framework

Every redesign follows this strategic sequence:

**Positioning → Before/after direction → Visual system → User journey → Distinctive brand ideas → Key page systems → Trust/proof → Mobile → Photography/content → Business outcomes → Client approvals → Inputs required → Next steps**

This sequence is reusable.

The answers inside it are not.

A dermatologist, dental clinic, cosmetic surgeon and injectable clinic should not come out looking or sounding identical simply because they went through the same process.

---

# 2. Rule Zero: Diagnose Before Designing

No one starts designing simply because the client says:

> "We want something more premium."

"Premium" means nothing until we understand what the clinic needs to communicate.

Before visual design, establish:

- What is the business?
- Which services actually drive revenue?
- Which services should drive future revenue?
- Who is the clinic trying to attract?
- What do those patients care about?
- What needs to be believed before someone books?
- What currently makes the clinic distinctive?
- What are the major trust barriers?
- Where does the clinic want to be in 2-3 years?
- What does the website need to do inside the wider acquisition system?

If the clinic wants to grow from two practitioners to ten, do not build a site where the founders effectively **are** the brand.

If the clinic wants to become known for regenerative medicine, do not bury regenerative care underneath a generic list of treatments.

**Architecture follows business strategy.**

**The design system follows the creative direction, not the other way around.**

**SEO and CRO are constraints to design within, not excuses for generic design.**

---

# 3. Evidence Before Preference

Before recommending a new direction, review the current asset.

Use:

**KEEP** — existing assets worth protecting.

**CHANGE** — things actively weakening positioning, trust, UX or conversion.

**MISSING** — assets/content required by the new strategy.

**UNCERTAIN** — things that require client confirmation.

Evidence can come from:

- Current website
- Mobile experience
- Analytics
- Search Console
- Google Business Profile
- Reviews
- Competitors
- Existing photography
- Treatment/service inventory
- Client discovery
- Search/SERP evidence
- Client-supplied data

Do not redesign from taste alone.

The strategist should be able to explain:

> "The existing site is good at X, currently creates perception Y, but the business needs to become Z. Therefore the redesign needs to do A, B and C."

If that is not clear, the team is not ready to design.

---

# 4. Positioning Thesis

Every project needs one primary design thesis.

Examples of a useful thesis:

> "Clinically authoritative first. Premium second."

> "Private dermatology expertise with hospitality-level service."

A weak thesis is:

> "Modern, luxurious and clean."

That could describe thousands of websites.

The positioning thesis should define:

- What we want people to think
- What we do not want them to think
- What the site should feel like
- What the clinic should be known for
- How premium is communicated
- Which commercial categories deserve greater visibility
- How the clinic brand relates to individual practitioners

Complete:

> "Within three seconds of landing, a prospective patient should understand that this is ______."

And:

> "It should never be mistaken for ______."

Turn positioning into visible decisions.

Use a simple comparison:

| Area | Current | Proposed |

| Colour | | |
| Typography | | |
| Photography | | |
| Navigation | | |
| Treatment discovery | | |
| Trust | | |
| CTAs | | |
| Practitioner positioning | | |

Every visual change should have a reason.

Do not say:

> "We prefer beige."

Say:

> "The existing dark luxury system signals beauty-led premium. A lighter clinical system shifts perception toward private medicine while retaining a premium feel."

---

# 6. Decision Hierarchy

Not every decision carries equal weight.

## Level 1 — Business strategy
Who are we? Who do we serve? Where are we going?

## Level 2 — Positioning
How should the market understand us?

## Level 3 — UX / information architecture
How do users find, evaluate and choose?

## Level 4 — Content / proof
What makes them believe us?

## Level 5 — Visual expression
How should this look and feel?

Lower-level preferences should not casually overturn higher-level strategic decisions.

A client saying "I don't personally like serif fonts" is Level 5 feedback.

It should not automatically undo a Level 2 decision to move the clinic from luxury beauty toward clinical medicine.

---

# 7. Visual System

Only after positioning is clear should the design language be defined.

Specify:

- Palette
- Typography
- Spacing
- Layout density
- Button hierarchy
- Card/container logic
- Iconography
- Photography language
- Motion
- Contrast

Internal design rules can be precise.

Client-facing language should communicate intent rather than arbitrary rules.

Internal:

> Gold ≈ 1%.

Client-facing:

> Gold is used sparingly as a brand accent.

The client should understand the principle without being asked to approve unnecessary implementation trivia.

---

# 8. User Journey

Design around how a patient thinks, not how a clinic organises its treatment inventory.

Most users arrive through one of three routes:

1. **I know the treatment I want.**
2. **I know the problem I want solved.**
3. **I do not know what I need yet.**

The website should accommodate all three.

Example:

**By treatment**  
Polynucleotides

**By concern**  
Tired-looking skin

**Guided route**  
Find your starting point

Every redesign must answer:

> "How does someone who does not understand aesthetic terminology find the right next step?"

If the answer is "scroll through 40 treatment names", the UX is not solved.

---

# 9. Distinctive Brand Ideas

Find 2-4 ideas the clinic can genuinely own.

These are not filler taglines.

They should express how the clinic operates, what it believes, or why a patient should choose it.

For every idea ask:

- Is it true?
- Is it differentiating?
- Can the website demonstrate it?
- Can the clinic deliver it operationally?
- Can it recur naturally across pages?
- Does it support the commercial position?

If not, do not use it.

A distinctive idea should shape UX/content architecture, not exist only as decorative copy.

---

# 10. Page Systems, Not Isolated Pages

Do not design 30 unrelated screens.

Build reusable systems.

Typical page types:

- Homepage
- Treatment/service
- Concern
- Practitioner profile
- Results/case
- About/team
- Consultation/booking
- Location, when relevant

Typical reusable components:

- Hero
- Trust/evidence strip
- Treatment card
- Concern card
- Practitioner card
- Results module
- Review/testimonial
- FAQ
- CTA
- Treatment facts
- Pricing
- Author/reviewer block
- Related treatments
- Internal navigation

The principle:

> **Design the system once. Populate it repeatedly.**

A new practitioner, service or location should not require a new design concept.

---

# 11. Brand Architecture

Founder-led clinics commonly create a strategic problem: the individual is stronger than the scalable clinic brand.

Determine explicitly:

- What does the clinic brand own?
- What do practitioners own?
- What belongs to treatment pages?
- What belongs to individual expert pages?
- What happens when new clinicians join?

Default growth architecture:

> **Clinic brand → services/treatments → clinical team → individual experts**

Individuals can build strong authority beneath the clinic without taking ownership of the entire commercial architecture.

Example:

Treatment page:

> Polynucleotides at [Clinic]

Practitioner page:

> Polynucleotides are one of Dr X's specialist treatment areas at [Clinic].

This keeps treatment equity and commercial authority centred on the scalable asset.

---

# 12. Trust & Proof System

Medical/aesthetic websites should **prove**, not merely claim.

Build a proof inventory.

Possible evidence includes:

- Registrations
- Qualifications
- Professional memberships
- Awards
- Review rating/count
- Review source
- Before/after results
- Case information
- Practitioner attribution
- Clinic location
- Years operating
- Relevant media
- Teaching/speaking
- Original clinical content
- Published expert commentary

Present evidence close to the claim it supports.

Avoid generic statements such as:

> "Award-winning experts delivering exceptional results."

Prefer:

- actual qualification
- actual award
- actual review
- actual result
- actual case

Never invent evidence for design completeness.

If an item has not been supplied, use a labelled placeholder or omit the section until real proof exists.

---

# 13. Photography System

Photography is part of the strategy.

Define:

- Hero photography
- Practitioner portraits
- Consultation imagery
- Clinic interiors
- Treatment preparation
- Treatment details
- Natural skin
- Before/after results

Create the shot list before the shoot.

Where temporary imagery is required during design, AI imagery may be used for **art-direction demonstration only**.

Never use fabricated:

- practitioner portraits
- patient results
- credentials
- clinical evidence

Label stand-ins clearly.

The finished site should not rely on "we'll replace the stock photography later" as an undefined task.

---

# 14. Mobile

Mobile is not desktop made narrower.

Review:

- Hero hierarchy
- Navigation
- CTA visibility
- Treatment browsing
- Forms
- Results
- Practitioner cards
- Typography
- Image crops
- Spacing
- Sticky actions where appropriate

Clinic websites often benefit from persistent access to:

- Call
- Book

Every major design approval should include representative mobile views.

---

# 15. Business Outcomes

Every redesign presentation must explain what the design is intended to improve commercially.

Examples:

- Faster understanding
- Greater trust
- Easier service discovery
- More consultation intent
- Better mobile behaviour
- Greater visibility for strategic treatment categories
- Better organic landing-page architecture
- Improved scalability
- Stronger practitioner/entity presentation
- More credible proof

Do not invent conversion uplift forecasts.

Instead define what will be measured after launch:

- Booking starts
- Booking completions
- CTA clicks
- Calls
- Forms
- Treatment-page engagement
- Organic landing-page traffic
- Relevant rankings
- Mobile conversion behaviour

Design serves the business.

---

# 16. Client Strategy Presentation

Target approximately 12-16 slides.

Recommended sequence:

1. Cover / positioning statement
2. The aim
3. Current → proposed
4. Business outcomes
5. Visual system
6. Homepage / first impression
7. User journey
8. Distinctive brand idea or clinic methodology
9. Priority category/service
10. Clinical team / brand architecture
11. Treatment/service page system
12. Trust/results
13. Mobile/booking
14. Photography/content
15. What we need the client to approve
16. Inputs + next steps

Rules:

- One idea per slide.
- One sentence explains why. The visual proves it.
- Mockups should be large.
- Avoid agency jargon.
- Do not present five arbitrary directions if there is enough evidence to recommend one.
- Do not ask the client to solve our design problem.

Instead of:

> "Which navigation approach do you prefer?"

Say:

> "We recommend concern + treatment navigation because patients arrive with both types of intent."

---

# 17. Client Feedback Classification

Client feedback must be classified before acting on it.

## Strategic
"This does not reflect our move into dermatology."

Revisit the strategy.

## Accuracy
"Dr Smith is not a dermatologist."

Correct immediately.

## User / UX
"Patients will not understand this wording."

Investigate and resolve.

## Compliance
"We cannot make that claim."

Correct immediately.

## Preference
"I do not personally like serif fonts."

Assess against strategy. Do not automatically redesign.

## New information
"We are opening another location."

Assess which previously approved decisions are genuinely affected.

This prevents subjective preference from overriding stronger strategic decisions by default.

---

# 18. Revision Control

Round 1:

**Strategic direction**

Round 2:

**Refinement following agreed strategic feedback**

After strategic approval:

Previously approved decisions are considered locked unless new information materially changes:

- business strategy
- factual accuracy
- user evidence
- technical feasibility
- compliance

A client should not be able to approve positioning on Monday and reopen the whole strategy Thursday because another stakeholder saw a colour they disliked.

---

# 19. Approval Gate

Never finish a presentation with:

> "Let us know what you think."

Ask the client to approve specific decisions.

Typical items:

- Overall positioning
- Visual direction
- User journey
- Treatment/service hierarchy
- Practitioner hierarchy
- Homepage structure
- Photography direction

Anything not approved remains out of full build.

Separate:

**approval required**

from:

**information required**

They are not the same thing.

---

# 20. Compliance as a Dedicated Gate

Because CosmediSites operates in a regulated category, compliance is not a generic QA bullet.

Check:

- Claims
- Credentials
- Practitioner titles
- Treatment terminology
- Before/after usage
- Testimonials
- Promotional language
- Pricing claims
- Guarantees/outcome language
- Patient consent

Claims should be substantiated rather than polished into something stronger than the evidence supports.

---

# 21. SEO Architecture Before Final Design

SEO should not be bolted on during development.

Before navigation/page architecture is locked, define:

- Existing URLs worth retaining
- Main service pages
- Concern pages
- Location structure
- Practitioner pages
- Supporting content
- Internal linking model
- Pages being merged
- Pages being removed
- Redirect requirements

A beautiful UX should not be approved before someone checks whether valuable search architecture is being destroyed.

---

# 22. Build Handoff

The designer should not hand the developer "some screens".

Handoff includes:

- Approved page designs
- Mobile designs
- Component library
- Typography
- Colour tokens
- Button states
- Image ratios
- Navigation behaviour
- CTA rules
- Content states
- Empty states
- Responsive behaviour
- Photography requirements
- SEO/content notes
- URL/redirect requirements
- Client-approved copy
- Tracking requirements

The developer should rarely be required to invent UX.

---

# 23. Internal Quality Test

Before showing a redesign to a client, give it to someone who did **not** work deeply on the design.

Give them 60 seconds.

They should be able to answer:

- What kind of clinic is this?
- What is it trying to be known for?
- What should the patient do next?
- What makes it credible?
- How would a patient find the right treatment?
- Does this feel designed for this business specifically?

If they cannot answer those questions, the design is not finished.

---

# 24. Post-Launch Learning

The process does not end at launch.

Review:

## 30 days
Technical and UX issues.

## 60 days
Behaviour and content observations.

## 90 days
Commercial and SEO performance.

Use:

**KEEP → IMPROVE → TEST → SCALE**

The operating system should improve from real project evidence, not from accumulating theory.

---

# 25. Biggest Failure Modes

Avoid:

- Palette swapping instead of repositioning
- Treating "premium" as black + gold
- Designing from the logo outward
- Stock photography as brand strategy
- Making founders bigger than the clinic despite expansion plans
- Treatment-menu-first UX
- Claims without proof
- Generic copy that could belong to any clinic
- Presenting too many options instead of making a recommendation
- Building pages individually instead of building a system
- Going into development before strategic approval
- Reopening locked strategy because of lower-level preferences
- Ending presentations without a clear decision
- Launching without measurement

---

# 26. Definition of a Successful CosmediSites Redesign

A successful project leaves the client with three things.

## A stronger position
The clinic is easier to understand and distinguish.

## A stronger acquisition asset
The website makes trust, discovery and conversion easier.

## A scalable system
New treatments, locations, content and practitioners can be added without breaking the brand or redesigning the site.

That is the actual product.

**Not the homepage.**
