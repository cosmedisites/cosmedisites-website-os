# Stage 3 — Positioning

**Purpose:** Define the market perception, brand architecture and strategic ideas the new website must make obvious.

## 3A — Generate Positioning Directions

**Use when:** After Strategic Diagnosis is approved internally.  
**Inputs:** Project Strategy Brief; Evidence Pack; Strategic Diagnosis.  
**Produces:** Up to 3 evidence-based positioning directions.

```text
Develop up to three credible positioning directions for the client.

Each direction must include:
- three-second perception;
- wrong perception to avoid;
- positioning thesis;
- reason it fits the commercial brief;
- proof the business can actually support;
- implications for service visibility;
- implications for practitioner / clinic hierarchy;
- future-growth fit;
- main risk.

Rules:
- Do not create options that differ only in tone or colours.
- One direction may be clearly strongest; say so.
- Do not invent a positioning that the clinic cannot operationally deliver.
- Avoid empty adjectives such as “premium, modern, trusted” unless made concrete.
```

## 3B — Select and Sharpen Positioning

**Use when:** When candidate directions exist.  
**Inputs:** Candidate directions; Project Strategy Brief; Strategic Diagnosis.  
**Produces:** Recommended Positioning Thesis.

```text
Evaluate the candidate positioning directions against:

Commercial fit
Patient clarity
Credibility / proof
Differentiation
Ability to support priority services
Ability to scale with new practitioners / locations
Search / content implications
Risk of wrong category perception

Choose the strongest direction.

Output:
CALL — recommended direction
WHY — 4–6 evidence-based reasons
THREE-SECOND PERCEPTION
WRONG PERCEPTION
POSITIONING THESIS
PRIORITY SERVICES / CATEGORIES
WHAT THE BRAND MUST OWN
WHAT PRACTITIONERS MAY OWN
WHAT THE DESIGN MUST NOT SIGNAL
OPEN QUESTIONS

Do not average multiple directions into a bland compromise.
```

## 3C — Brand Architecture

**Use when:** When the business has founders, multiple practitioners, locations or expansion plans.  
**Inputs:** Positioning Thesis; growth model; current team; service ownership.  
**Produces:** Brand architecture rules.

```text
Define the brand architecture for the redesigned site.

Answer:

1. What does the clinic brand own?
2. What can individual practitioners own?
3. What do locations own?
4. Who owns treatment authority?
5. Who owns patient results?
6. How should authorship / clinical review work?
7. How should future clinicians be added?
8. What happens if a founder leaves?
9. What should treatment pages say about providers?
10. What should practitioner profiles say about treatments?

Output:
CLINIC OWNS
PRACTITIONERS OWN
LOCATIONS OWN
SHARED PROOF
INTERNAL LINKING RELATIONSHIP
NAMING RULES
SCALABILITY RULES
DO NOT DO

Optimise for a durable clinic asset unless the commercial brief explicitly calls for a personality-led brand.
```

## 3D — Positioning Validator

**Use when:** Before client-facing positioning approval.  
**Inputs:** Positioning Thesis; Brand Architecture; Strategic Diagnosis.  
**Produces:** PASS/FAIL validation.

```text
Validate the proposed positioning.

Tests:
- Can it be understood in one sentence?
- Is it meaningfully different from generic “premium clinic” language?
- Is it supported by real proof or operational reality?
- Does it solve the diagnosis?
- Does it improve discovery / trust / booking logic?
- Does it support priority services?
- Does it survive the clinic doubling its team?
- Does the clinic remain stronger than any individual practitioner where that is the stated growth model?
- Does it create any compliance risk?
- Could another clinic use the same wording unchanged?

For each test return PASS / FAIL / BLOCKED.

If it passes, write the final approved internal thesis in no more than 25 words.
```

## 3E — Client-Facing Positioning Explanation

**Use when:** When preparing the client deck or strategy review.  
**Inputs:** Approved Positioning Thesis; Current → Proposed evidence.  
**Produces:** Client-facing explanation.

```text
Translate the approved positioning into client language.

Produce:
1. A slide headline.
2. A one-sentence objective.
3. “Feels like” — 2–3 concrete category references.
4. “Never mistaken for” — 2–3 wrong categories/perceptions.
5. “Premium through” — explain how premium is communicated.
6. One paragraph explaining why this direction is commercially useful.

Rules:
- No SEO jargon.
- No internal labels like “entity architecture”.
- No unverified claim.
- Do not turn the strategy into vague brand poetry.
```
